Test — Torque Balance

testGuide
2:30 PM · Sun, Sep' 20 2026
FAIL
Target
Every tick, derive current total torque on the body and fire RCS with exactly the opposing torque. See if RCS alone can hold AoA ≈ 0 during a max-throttle launch with gimbal fixed at 0.
Approach
targetTorque = −τ_total (each snapshot, no memory)
GuideRCS.targetTorqueRcs() converts to per-pod duty.
What happened
Limit cycle. Jump → zero → jump → zero at ~2 Hz on the telemetry torque trace, until RCS saturated, then a monotonic drift to crash.
Why it failed
  • Deadband release: below 100 N·m the guide sent cmdRcsDuty(null), relinquishing control. Next snapshot saw torque back and re-fired. Classic bang-bang limit cycle.
  • Snapshot rate mismatch: 20 Hz throttle (later fixed to 80 Hz) put guidance 4 physics ticks behind.
  • Snapshot is one tick stale: the torque it reacts to has already evolved before the command lands.

Predictive Torque Cancel

predictiveTorque
3:00 PM · Sun, Sep' 20 2026
FAIL
Target
Instead of reacting to past torque, predict the next tick's drag torque from current state and command RCS to cancel it before it happens.
Approach
predict state at t+dt (no RCS assumed)
τ_drag_next = derive(predicted state).torqueDrag
targetTorque = −τ_drag_next
fire RCS via GuideRCS
What happened
Ran for a while looking great — torque trace smooth and low. Then attitude suddenly broke away and rocket crashed.
Why it failed
  • Prediction assumes no RCS, but RCS is firing. Each shot shifts lateral velocity → AoA grows → drag torque prediction goes stale by the time command lands.
  • Small residual (200–500 N·m) per tick in a CP-above-CoM instability compounds exponentially.
  • Pure feedforward has no rate/attitude feedback to absorb drift.
feedforward-only positive-feedback model-mismatch

Add classical PID on top of feedforward — proportional attitude hold (vs local vertical), rate damping, and an integral term to absorb slow biases.

Gimbal Attitude Control Experiments

Gimbal: 2-tick Predictive

gimbalPredictive2
6:35 PM · Sun, Sep' 20 2026
ACCURATE WIN
Target
Use the engine gimbal (not RCS) to autonomously cancel drag torque during ascent. Match predicted future-tick drag torque with gimbal torque by commanding the correct rate before the tick where it needs to act.
Key insight
Physics integrates gimbal rate one tick at a time. Guidance replaces the rate every snapshot. So a "2-tick pipeline" only needs to compute the rate that lands gimbal on target at the next tick — predicting two ticks ahead and issuing a rate for tick N+2 caused repeated overshoot/undershoot because the rate was overwritten before it finished acting.
Approach
// current tick N
g_N = current gimbal angle
g_N1 = g_N + R_N·dt                       // committed
S_N1 = predict(S_N, gimbal = g_N1)
τ_drag_N1 = derive(S_N1).torqueDrag

// Solve gimbal torque: A·cos(g) + B·sin(g) = −τ_drag_N1
g_req = asin(−τ_drag_N1 / R_amp) − φ
g_req = clamp(g_req, ±GIMBAL_MAX_DEG)

// Rate so gimbal lands on g_req at N+1
R_cmd = clamp((g_req − g_N)/dt, ±GIMBAL_RATE_DEG_S)
send(setGimbalRate(R_cmd))
Result
Rock solid. Total torque held at 100–150 N·m at low altitude, then as the rocket climbed and drag faded, held steady at 1 N·m for over a minute. Attitude never drifted more than a fraction of a degree. No RCS, no propellant waste.
Why it works
  • Gimbal is continuous — one rate command integrates smoothly within the tick. No pulsed duty-cycle timing mismatch like RCS.
  • Prediction is exact (proven by predictVerifier), so the solved g_req matches the actual torque requirement.
  • 1-tick horizon correctly matches the pipeline: predict, solve, command — all in the same tick, gimbal ready by next.
  • Angle and rate clamps handled gracefully; saturation simply caps the achievable torque rather than destabilising.

Coast-Phase Control · Shelved

Coast-Phase Drag Cancel — Shelved

coastDrag · abandoned
7:20 PM · Sun, Sep' 20 2026
FAIL · SHELVED
Target
Extend the working gimbalPredictive2 controller past burnout, into the coast phase — hold AoA ≈ 0 while the rocket is still airborne but no longer thrusting, so apogee and landing stay clean.
Approaches tried
  • Straight port of gimbalPredictive2 into coast — worked for a few seconds, then attitude broke away.
  • N-tick horizon (2, 3, 4 ticks) — no improvement; overshoot and undershoot alternated. Longer horizon made it worse, not better.
  • PID assist on top — masked the drift briefly, then saturated the gimbal.
  • RCS assist — reintroduced the Phase-1 RCS limit-cycle problem verbatim.
What happened
In every variant the gimbal ended up wobbling visibly — angle trace oscillated a few degrees peak-to-peak at ~1–2 Hz, which reads as unstable on the plot even when the numeric AoA stays bounded. And crucially, coast drag is not a tame continuation of ascent drag: once thrust is off, torque direction and magnitude swing hard with tiny attitude changes. Small model error → large response.
Why we're shelving it
  • We can hand-tune any single launch profile to survive coast. We cannot write universal logic that saves every case — the drag regime during rest is fundamentally different and no single controller covers it.
  • The gimbal wobble in every near-working variant is a symptom, not a cosmetic issue: the controller is fighting an unmodelled effect, and it will keep fighting it no matter the gain.
  • Continuing to tune would just be Phase 1 again — treating the symptom of a deeper mismatch.
Decision
Abandon coast-phase active attitude control for now. Accept AoA drift during coast. Revisit only if a coast-phase physical model is added to the sim (drag-regime separation, turbulence term, or a real aerodynamic coefficient table). Until then, no controller will be honest about what it can hold.
What survives
gimbalPredictive2 stays as the ascent-only controller — that part genuinely works and remains the accurate win. Coast is a separate problem and gets its own future experiment, not a bolt-on to this one.

Ascent Attitude Control

Ascent: AoA Hold with Analytic Derivatives

ascentAoaHold
6:45 AM· Tue, Sep' 22 2026
ACCURATE WIN
Target
Deliver a full-ascent attitude controller that keeps the rocket's angle-of-attack bounded through the entire burn — from pad to Max-Q to post-Max-Q — with no hand-tuning per launch profile. RCS was already ruled out; the gimbal was the only viable actuator (proven by gimbalPredictive2). The question was whether a controller on top of it could ride through the drag torque peak without hand-tuned gains.
Iterations

This guide went through six distinct iterations before it settled. Each one exposed a specific failure mode:

  • v1 — PUSH + COAST + HOLD, finite-difference derivatives. Straight port of the sin-pulse prefix idea from predictivePlusAoAPush, with a new HOLD phase triggered on AoA ≥ 0. ω_AoA and α_AoA came from 1-tick and 2-tick backward finite differences. Immediate failure: 1/dt² = 6400× amplification turned every rounding error in AoA into a gimbal-saturating torque. Attitude broke within 2 seconds of HOLD entry.
  • v2 — analytic derivatives. Replaced both derivatives with exact algebra on body-frame velocity + acceleration: ω_AoA = ω + P/r² and α_AoA = α − 2PQ/r⁴. No more amplification. HOLD became tractable — but the rate damper alone wasn't enough post-Max-Q: the gimbal kept chasing a growing AoA as dynamic pressure fell.
  • v3 — HOLD trigger with ω_AoA gate. Entry was originally AoA ≥ 0 AND ω_AoA > 0. Backfired — the ω gate fired late in most runs (ω sometimes crossing zero *before* AoA did), so HOLD missed the transition and CoAST continued past the window. Reverted to AoA-only entry; the ω condition is still in the code but commented out.
  • v4 — COASTnAoADAMP phase. Reverting HOLD → COAST on AoA < 0 caused a hard discontinuity — HOLD's damped torque snapped to zero, and the gimbal slammed. Added a third phase in between: a scaled position controller that decays AoA back toward zero without the accel-cancel term. Formula went through several iterations; the final shape is a critically-damped second-order position response, τ = −I·16·AoA / K².
  • v5 — τ_dQ east nudge. Post-Max-Q the rate damper was fighting drag with diminishing effect — the pressure peak passed but the AoA kept creeping positive. Added a soft, always-east bias that only activates when dQ < 0: τ_dQ = −K_dQ · I · 0.5(1 − tanh(dQ/Q_ref)). Tuned down from 0.05/5000 to 0.005/1000 after the first pass rotated too aggressively into the falling-pressure window.
  • v6 — useNow sub-mode inside HOLD. The pre-Max-Q and post-Max-Q regimes want opposite things: rising pressure wants next-tick prediction, falling pressure wants current-tick reaction. Added a runtime switch inside HOLD: dQ ≥ 0 uses the N+1 predicted derivatives, dQ < 0 uses current-tick. This was the change that closed the loop.

Final tuning: INITIAL_COAST_S 5 → 4.9, PUSH_T_S 5 → 4.8, and 16 / HOLD_K_DAMP in the COASTnAoADAMP formula split into two independent constants (COAST_DAMP_GAIN, COAST_DAMP_K) so the two phases tune separately.

Key insight
Finite differences of AoA derivatives amplify by 1/dt² — at dt = 1/80 s, that's 6400×. Any noise in AoA gets amplified into a gimbal-saturating torque. The fix wasn't a better filter or a shorter horizon — it was not using finite differences at all.

With AoA = atan2(u, v) in body-frame velocity components, the derivatives are exact algebra:

u = relVx·cosθ + relVy·sinθ
v = −relVx·sinθ + relVy·cosθ
a_x = a_Ix·cosθ + a_Iy·sinθ
a_y = −a_Ix·sinθ + a_Iy·cosθ
r² = u² + v²
P = v·a_x − u·a_y
Q = u·a_x + v·a_y

ω_AoA = ω + P/r²
α_AoA = α_body − 2·P·Q/r⁴
No 1/dt², no amplification, no filter needed.
Final architecture
Four phases, one gimbal rate command per tick, all built on top of the gimbalPredictive2 drag-cancel core: 
// PRE_COAST (4.9 s) — straight climb, drag cancel only
τ_desired = 0

// PUSH (4.8 s) — sin-pulse east kick
τ = EAST_SIGN · I · A · sin(2πt/T),  A = 2π·Δθ/T²

// COAST — pure drag cancel, AoA drifts negative → positive
τ_desired = 0

// HOLD — triggered when AoA ≥ 0
τ_desired = −I · α_AoA            // accel cancel
          − K · I · ω_AoA         // rate damper
          − K_dQ · I · f(dQ)      // post-Max-Q east nudge
          // f(dQ) = 0.5·(1 − tanh(dQ/Q_ref)), always ≥ 0

// Throttle program (independent of phase)
alt < 8 km          → 100%
8 ≤ alt < 13 km    → 70%   // Max-Q throttle bucket
alt ≥ 13 km         → 100%
Every value from the derived state at the predicted next tick — nothing finite-differenced. The gimbal rate solve is the same algebraic form as gimbalPredictive2: solve τ_gimbal(g) = τ_desired − τ_drag_next for g_req, clamp, convert to rate.
Why it works
  • Analytic derivatives — no 1/dt² amplification, so the HOLD torque terms respond to actual AoA motion, not to numerical noise.
  • Predicted next-tick values throughout HOLD — no stale actuals, no phase lag. The gimbal lands on target exactly when it's needed, not one tick late.
  • dQ-driven east nudge — a soft, always-east bias that grows only when dynamic pressure is falling (post-Max-Q). Fills the gap the rate damper leaves open, without ever rotating the wrong way.
  • Gimbal, not RCS — continuous actuator, smooth integration, no duty-cycle timing to fight.
Tuned constants
INITIAL_COAST_S:       4.9
PUSH_T_S:              4.8
PUSH_MAX_GIMBAL_DEG:   1.45
PUSH_EAST_SIGN:        -1
HOLD_K_DAMP:           4.0
HOLD_MAX_AOA_DEG:      8
HOLD_K_DQ:             0.005
HOLD_Q_REF:            1000
COAST_DAMP_GAIN:       16
COAST_DAMP_K:          4.0
THROTTLE_FRAC:         1.0
THROTTLE_ALT_LOW_KM:   8
THROTTLE_ALT_HIGH_KM:  13
THROTTLE_FRAC_LOW:     0.7
Result
Attitude held through the full ascent. AoA stayed bounded through Max-Q without any per-profile tuning. Torque trace clean, gimbal rate stayed well inside its ±40°/s envelope, no RCS involvement at any point. A 70% throttle bucket across 8–13 km (the Max-Q window) further softened the drag-torque peak without any destabilising effect on the AoA loop. This is the second fully accurate result after gimbalPredictive2 — and the first that covers the full burn rather than just the low-drag early phase.
Drawbacks
  • Not wind-ready. Every torque term derives from body-frame relative velocity, but the constants were tuned entirely with wind off. Once wind is enabled, the phase machine will need re-tuning — the PUSH amplitude and the HOLD damping gains both have explicit q_eff (relative dynamic pressure) baked into their behaviour, and wind shifts that. No wind-compensation term exists yet.
  • Constants are hand-tuned, not auto-tuned. INITIAL_COAST_S, PUSH_T_S, PUSH_DELTA_DEG, HOLD_K_DAMP, HOLD_K_DQ, HOLD_Q_REF all sit at values specific to this F9-class mass, thrust, and drag profile. A different stack would need different values. No trajectory-to-constants mapping exists — there's no way to hand the guide a target apogee / downrange / Max-Q and have it compute its own gains.

Full-Mission Experiments

LEO Insertion — Full Mission

leoInsertion
— · Tue, Sep 23
ACCURATE UP TO MECO · FAILS AT ORBIT
Target
Chain the ascent guide into a full mission: launch → MECO → stage separation → attitude slew → circularization burn → satellite in LEO. One guide, one phase machine, every hand-off autonomous. The goal wasn't just "get up there" — it was orbit insertion.
Iterations

The ascent phase worked from day one (it delegates to ascentAoaHold's proven control law). Everything after MECO went through six rewrites:

  • v1 — RCS-slew rotation via sin-pulse. The target was a 20° steep-up angle, delivered by RCS using the same τ = I·A·sin(2πt/T) formula as the PUSH pulse. Undershot by exactly half. RCS torque is ~1/280th of what's needed to slew an upper stage at that rate.
  • v2 — sin-pulse handed to the gimbal. Same formula, actuator swapped. Rotation worked, but the stage was coasting up at steep angle while the burn was supposed to take over — apogee kept rising past target during the slew.
  • v3 — dropped coast phase entirely. Separation → immediate rotate → immediate burn. Removed the "Karman-line coast" that was wasting altitude gain. Rotation target changed to horizontal (90° from local vertical) — the natural prograde direction at apogee.
  • v4 — bang-bang rotation. Replaced the sin-pulse with time-optimal bang-bang: fire max torque one way until the midpoint of the rotation, then max the other way to decelerate. RCS at saturation for the entire slew, but exits with zero residual ω. Tolerances were the tricky part — the exit-condition band had to be tight enough to catch the hand-off cleanly.
  • v5 — coast-to-apogee planning block. At cutoff, compute apogee, V_apogee (h/r_a), V_orbital (√(GM/r_a)), Δv, required fuel via Tsiolkovsky, burn duration, and time-to-apogee via Kepler. Everything the circularization phase needs, locked once so the coast tick doesn't re-derive.
  • v6 — net-zero-force RCS hold. During the 100+ second coast, the attitude hold was drifting the trajectory — RCS duty doesn't inherently balance linear force. Added targetTorqueRcsNoNetForce(): prioritises the lateral nozzle pair (larger moment arm), and only engages verticals when lateral torque alone can't meet demand, always in balanced pairs. Net linear force is exactly zero by construction.
Final architecture
// Phase machine — one switch, one tick per phase
ASCENT              → delegate to _hTick (ascentAoaHold's full machine)
MECO_SPOOL          → wait for separation to complete
SEPARATED_AXIAL     → booster RCS 'dn' + stage RCS 'up' till 10 m gap
                    → pneumatic pusher (2 m/s² for 1 s on discarded body)
ROTATE_BANG_BANG    → RCS max torque, mid-point reversal, exit at
                       target tilt = −10° from local vertical, ω ≈ 0
TARGET_APOGEE       → full throttle + gimbal attitude hold,
                       throttle decay inside 10 km margin,
                       cutoff at apogee ≥ 180 km
COAST_ROTATE        → bang-bang RCS slew to horizontal (θ_rel = −90°)
COAST_HOLD          → hold horizontal via targetTorqueRcsNoNetForce,
                       wait until t_to_apogee ≤ 0.25 · T_burn
CIRCULARIZE         → full throttle + gimbal hold horizontal,
                       throttle decay near V_orb, cutoff at v ≥ V_orb
DONE

Every torque demand that runs during coast goes through the net-force-free RCS distributor. Every burn-phase attitude hold uses the gimbal (100× more torque than RCS). Guidance reads the same derived state for every phase — no physics internal reach-through.

Result — up to MECO
Rock solid. Ascent runs to apogee 150 km on ascentAoaHold's control law. MECO trigger fires on osculating apogee, not altitude — the physically meaningful hand-off. Separation is clean: 10 m axial gap in seconds, booster drifts away, stage stays upright. Rotation to −10° completes with the bang-bang slew. Apogee burn hits 180 km within the throttle-decay margin. This whole sequence is reproducible across runs.
What failed — circularization
The circularization burn never actually reaches orbital velocity. Coast → rotate → burn all fire on schedule, the guidance status reads CIRCULARIZE, and the burn starts. But the required Δv is delivered in a fraction that is many times longer than the ideal Δv / ṁ. The upper stage runs out of burn time or drifts off the intended altitude before v ≥ V_orbital is achieved.
Why it failed
The mission profile is fundamentally wrong for an orbital insertion. "Steep up se orbit height tak jaldi pahunch ke koi fayeda nahi kyunki fir V_orbital achieve karne mein Δv ke times se 4–5× time lagta hai kyunki gravity exist karti hai."

Getting to the target altitude fast with a steep climb leaves almost all of the horizontal velocity to be added at the top. But that horizontal burn is fighting gravity the whole time — every second of horizontal thrust loses ~9.7 m/s of useful velocity to the vertical pull that the wings (which don't exist) would have countered. Gravity losses turn a 1.5 km/s ideal Δv into a burn that takes 4–5× longer than the mass-flow math predicts. By the time the required velocity arrives, apogee has dropped below the target altitude, and the resulting orbit is elliptical, not circular.

The real fix isn't a better circularization controller — it's a different ascent profile. Real launchers pitch over early and build horizontal velocity throughout the climb, arriving at orbit altitude with most of V_orbital already banked. That's the whole point of a gravity turn. This mission never made one.

Drawbacks
  • Ascent profile is steep, not orbital. Climb-to-altitude-then-burn-horizontal is the wrong shape. It maximises altitude gain per unit time and minimises velocity gain, which is the opposite of what orbit needs.
  • Gravity losses during the horizontal burn are unmodelled. The burn planning (Tsiolkovsky + ṁ) assumes ideal Δv, not gravity-and-steering losses. A 4–5× time multiplication is the observable consequence.
  • No pitch-program rework attempted. Everything after MECO was tuned, but MECO itself fires on the ascent profile inherited from ascentAoaHold — which was never designed to leave the stage in a good energy state for orbit.

LEO Insertion V2 — Full Mission

leoInsertionV2
— · 23 Sept' 2026
FULL MISSION · PAYLOAD IN ORBIT
Target
One guide, one phase machine, autonomous pad-to-orbit-and-deploy. Ascent → MECO → separation → burn to apogee → RCS boost → coast → circularization burn → coast to apogee → release payload → DONE. Every hand-off handled by guidance, no manual commands after launch.
What v1 got wrong
v1 climbed steeply to apogee, coasted, then tried to add all the horizontal velocity in a short burn near the top. That profile fights gravity the whole way — every second of horizontal thrust loses vertical velocity it didn't need to spend. The result was the same floor on every run: apogee locked at 209 km regardless of target, because the available burn never delivered V_orbital and the resulting orbit was elliptical.

The failure is a shape failure, not a controller failure. Ascent controller, separation logic, coast attitude hold — all worked. The mission had just been aimed at the wrong trajectory.

The road to v2
  • Coast was wasting altitude. Sitting at target altitude before burning meant the horizontal burn fought gravity for a 4-5× longer window than the mass-flow math suggested. Fix: stop coasting, keep burning — the burn itself is the ride to apogee.
  • Attitude drift during long coast. RCS torque without force-balancing walks the orbit off course over 100+ seconds. Fix: targetTorqueRcsNoNetForce — pairs lateral and vertical fires so ΣF = 0 is exact by construction, while still delivering the requested torque.
  • Apogee overshoot on shutdown spool. Merlin-class engines take ~2 s to actually stop; during that window thrust continues and adds ~30 m/s, which at orbital velocity moves apogee by ~50 km. Prediction lookahead reads the engine's own shutdownDurationS and cuts early by the predicted Δv.
  • Apogee target too imprecise at orbital velocity. One guidance tick moves apogee by 100+ km — "apogee ≥ target" was firing way past the mark. Replaced with a two-stage trim: main engine cuts at apogee ≥ target − margin, then RCS_BOOST does fine prograde push until apogee hits exactly.
  • Payload release timing. Releasing at apogee needs precise attitude and orbit. Fix: re-derive the apogee point from the eccentricity vector every tick and hold the nose along the local velocity there. Release triggers on vr sign flip — the exact apogee crossing.
Key insights

1. Attitude lock at 85° during the orbital burn. During STAGE_BURN, once the body's tilt-from-local-vertical reaches 85° (near-horizontal), the gimbal control switches from AoA-damping to a PD hold at that exact tilt. Below the threshold the nose stays velocity-aligned (natural gravity turn); above it, thrust points mostly tangentially, which is what actually raises perigee efficiently.

2. Two burn stages — main engine coarse, RCS fine. The main engine brings apogee near the target (coarse, cheap). RCS_BOOST then does the last ~100 m/s of perigee trim if the engine's spool overshoot left it short. The mission is not all-or-nothing: the engine lands close, and RCS closes the gap.

3. Spool compensation is physics, not tuning. Both the STAGE_BURN predictive cutoff and the CIRCULARIZE cutoff read the engine's own shutdownDurationS and compute the Δv that will be delivered during spool-down. The cutoff fires that much early, so the engine reaches zero thrust exactly when the vehicle reaches the target velocity. No hand-tuned margin.

4. Payload kick gets pre-compensated. The deploy mechanism adds a fixed 3 m/s prograde impulse to the payload. The stage only needs to reach V_orbital − kick, so the CIRCULARIZE cutoff fires (spool_Δv + kick) early. The stage ends up 3 m/s short — the payload ends up exactly on the target.

Phase machine
// Post-MECO path — v2 diverges from v1 here
ASCENT              → _hTick (ascentAoaHold core)
MECO_SPOOL          → trigger on osculating apogee ≥ 150 km
SEPARATED_AXIAL     → RCS push apart, pneumatic pusher, 10 m gap
STAGE_BURN          → full throttle, gimbal control:
                       pre-85°  : COASTnAoADAMP (velocity-aligned)
                       post-85° : PD hold at locked 85° tilt
                    → cutoff at apogee ≥ target, predictive spool
RCS_BOOST           → engine off, wait for spool, then 4× up nozzles
                       prograde until apogee hits target exactly
COAST_ROTATE        → bang-bang RCS slew to fixed inertial θ that
                       matches velocity direction at apogee
COAST_HOLD          → hold that θ via net-zero-force RCS,
                       wait for burn trigger
CIRCULARIZE         → full throttle, PD hold same θ
                    → cutoff when v_err ≤ (spool_Δv + payload_kick)
DEPLOY_PAYLOAD      → coast to apogee, hold nose along velocity at
                       the apogee point (recomputed every tick)
                    → release at vr sign flip
DONE                → RCS holds attitude indefinitely

Engine-side values (spool, Ve, mass flow) are read at runtime from the active engine object, not hardcoded — swapping the thruster type in the fleet editor carries through without touching the guide. Everything is tunable at runtime via Guidance.setLeoInsertionV2({...}).

Result
Payload is in orbit. The mission now runs autonomously end-to-end: ascent → MECO → separation → burn to apogee → RCS boost → coast → circularization burn → coast to apogee → release payload → DONE. Every hand-off handled by guidance, no manual commands after launch. Final orbit lands at the target altitude, the payload separates with the correct prograde kick, and the stage settles into a stable attitude-hold state for as long as the sim runs. This is the first guide that gets not just the vehicle but the payload into orbit.
Drawbacks
  • Sun and Moon gravity not modelled. The sim treats Earth as the only gravitating body. Over the timescales this mission runs (minutes to hours) the perturbation is negligible, but long-term orbit prediction would need a proper multi-body integration.

Lessons That Survived Every Iteration
Actuator choice
Continuous actuators (gimbal) are easier to control precisely than pulsed/duty-cycle actuators (RCS). Latency is baked into RCS at the hardware level; gimbal integrates smoothly.
Horizon
A control horizon must match the actuator's actual integration step. Predicting 2 ticks ahead when the rate gets overwritten every tick just causes oscillation. 1-tick pipeline is correct for the current architecture.
Prediction > feedback
Once prediction is verified accurate, feedforward-solve beats PID for this problem. PID's job was to mask the deeper problem (model mismatch); with a correct model, PID has nothing left to do.
Diagnose before tuning
Five failures were spent tuning PID gains while the actual culprit was either sign conventions, horizon mismatch, or actuation latency. predictVerifier should have come first.
Know when to stop
If a controller only works because it was hand-tuned to one launch profile, that isn't a controller — it's a lookup table. Shelve the approach, scope what actually works (ascent-only gimbal), and let the rest wait until the physics model catches up.
